How it works

Each dot is an MP, placed in one 3D space built from how they spoke on the subjects in Commons debates. Rotate it by dragging; click a dot for that MP's positions.

Pick anything in the list on the left. A line is one proposition (a bill or measure with MPs on both sides) and points towards where MPs who argued for sit. A disc is a subject, spanned by its propositions' lines. A group shows its subjects' discs. Dots are coloured by their score on what you picked; grey means no score.

Faint, dashed lines mean the space only partly explains that proposition. The fit strength is measured in a map rebuilt without that subject, so it is an honest test, and the page says "weak fit" when the picture should not be trusted.

In an MP's profile, positions in propositions are the two-sided evidence behind the map. Took a side lists speeches on propositions where only one side was found. They are shown for context and are not used to place anyone on the map.

Use View to adjust the display, Filters to fade parties or unplaced MPs, and the box at the top to find an MP. The browser Back button steps back through what you opened.

Find my place (top tabs) asks questions about real propositions and places a gold star for you. Relationships shows which subjects go together.

Near-duplicate propositions on the same bill are merged before anything is scored.
