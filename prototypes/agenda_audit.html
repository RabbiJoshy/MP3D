Why the original “defining” score can mislead

Mean absolute pairwise correlation includes sibling questions about one policy area, varies with which MPs spoke, and rewards party sorting. A minority position can still strongly define a group. A 50/50 split can be unpredictable from everything else and still deserve a place in the questionnaire.

Next: hold out whole debate families and future periods; compare party-only, global stance and party-plus-residual stance models. Evaluate voter transfer with shared proposal wording, not party identity.

A cheap discovery funnel

01
Freeze time and splits before discovery

Group near-duplicate passages, amendments and debate families. Keep future debates and hand labels sealed. Store corpus/version hashes.

02
Learn topics; retain the long tail

Start with the existing TF–IDF → SVD → clusters. Compare 60/110/180 topics and bootstrap stability. Detect procedural clusters; never require a vote. Use soft or multi-topic membership for fiscal passages scattered across bills.

03
Measure attention before screening sides

Word share, unique speaker-days, debate breadth and persistence. Report chamber, committee and minister/backbench strata separately. Winsorise prolific speakers. The chart currently shows cached passage share only.

04
Write actions, then find opposition

A writer extracts up to two grounded actions from diverse passages. Retrieve support and opposition across the whole corpus, using lexical + SVD similarity. Do not choose “opposing MPs” by party; require opposing evidence on the same action. Abstain when it is a shared goal or two different policies.

05
Screen → calibrate → expand

Jev reads 24 independent MP/debate examples per candidate. Support, oppose, conditional, neither and ambiguous are separate outcomes. Calibrate on sealed human labels; both-high scores abstain. Expand only proposals with multiple independent MPs and debates per side.

06
Select a portfolio, not a league table

Keep attention, public salience, stance quality and incremental separation visible. Deduplicate near-identical questions. Greedy information gain with topic coverage and a novelty reserve; log choices and show sensitivity to all weights.

Better maths, in order

First: masked ideal points

Pr(supportᵢⱼ) = sigmoid(aⱼᵀzᵢ − bⱼ)

Fit observed MP–proposal stances only. Item direction a, difficulty b, MP position z. Shrink sparse items; choose 1–6 dimensions by held-out prediction, not an imposed 3D truth. Align rotations for display. Keep 3D as a projection if higher rank wins.

Then: separate speaking from believing

Pr(mentionsᵢⱼ) ≠ Pr(supportᵢⱼ | mentions)

A hurdle/Poisson attention model with speech exposure, role and topic effects describes who talks. It cannot identify silent MPs’ views without assumptions. Topic emphasis is not perceived issue ownership: survey “best party to handle” questions are needed for that.

Most interesting: cross-party coalitions

zᵢ = party mean + individual residual

Compare against a party-free model; never present residual space as the whole ideology. Look for stable residual clusters across time, not just a rebellion count.

Adaptive questions

gainⱼ ≈ ½ log(1 + pⱼ(1−pⱼ) aⱼᵀΣaⱼ)

Binary answers make information depend on the current estimated answer probability. Separate “how much I care” from response reliability; caring should weight matching, not arbitrarily tighten positional uncertainty.

External salience, honestly

YouGov tracker: prompted, up to three issues. Date-align waves; map discovered topics many-to-many with recorded uncertainty. This is an external selection prior, not fully unsupervised discovery.

BES panel and open-ended responses: weighted public issue coverage and, where suitable questions overlap, voter positions. Downloads require login. Match wording and wave-specific weights; keep national salience separate from personal priorities.

Methods: Text-Based Ideal Points is a text-only comparator, but vocabulary can encode framing rather than stance. Bayesian ideal points support a probabilistic alternative to filled-table SVD.
